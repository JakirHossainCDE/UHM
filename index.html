About this project

Urban Heat Mapping

Urban Heat Mapping (UHM) is a project by Amna Azeem and Md Jakir Hossain, exploring how interactive maps can make urban weather and environmental conditions easier to understand.

What am I looking at?

The map requests modelled air temperature at 2 m, humidity, wind at 10 m and forecasts from Open-Meteo. Air quality uses CAMS forecasts via Open-Meteo, including U.S. AQI. Model times and retrieval times are shown separately. Missing values are never treated as zero.

What should I not infer?

This is not a sensor network, a satellite thermal map or a validated urban heat-island analysis. Weather model resolution varies by region; air-quality grids are approximately 11 km in Europe and 45 km globally. Zooming in does not increase data resolution. The satellite basemap provides visual context only; it is not live imagery or temperature data.

How do the indicator layers work?

The visible area is sampled at 16 locations. Each coloured display cell shows the model estimate at its centre; its edges are display boundaries, not actual weather-model grid boundaries. Values are not interpolated. Nearby samples may share a model cell. Pan or zoom to update the layer automatically, or turn off Auto-update and use Refresh layer. Switch indicators without downloading the same area again. Hover over a cell for details; click a value to inspect its forecast.

Temperature is at 2 m; humidity is relative humidity; wind speed is at 10 m and arrows point in the direction the air is moving. Rain shows total precipitation in millimetres over the preceding hour, including rain, showers and snow water equivalent. Forecast charts show precipitation probability separately. Air quality uses the U.S. AQI scale. A dash or unfilled cell means no data.

Forecasts, comparison & downloads

Forecasts show the next 24 hours or seven daily summaries. All displayed times belong to the selected place; CSV exports include UTC, local time, model-grid coordinates and retrieval time. Comparison cards are snapshots: refresh a card to update it. Forecasts are informative estimates, not official warnings or guidance for safety-critical decisions.

Privacy & service availability

No account is needed. Your position is requested only when you choose “My location” and allow browser permission. Coordinates are sent to weather providers, searches to Open-Meteo / GeoNames, and visible map tiles to the chosen map provider. Sharing a view includes its coordinates in the link. Only your theme preference is stored locally; comparison places are kept in memory for this visit. External services may be delayed or unavailable.

Project & sources

The original project report (PDF, 3.7 MB) documents the early concept, including proposed features that are not implemented here. The current website runs entirely in the browser; it has no Python data-processing backend.

Weather: Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0). Air quality: Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service via Open-Meteo. Maps: OpenStreetMap contributors, Esri & imagery contributors, OpenTopoMap. Interactive mapping: Leaflet.

Light and dark maps use OpenStreetMap with display styling and need no API key. Satellite and topographic maps remain optional. Map availability is subject to each provider’s terms. Report a map issue or contact the project.
